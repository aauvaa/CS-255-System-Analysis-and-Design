# CS-255-System-Analysis-and-Design

Portfolio repository for CS 255: System Analysis and Design at Southern New Hampshire University.

**Briefly summarize the DriverPass project. Who was the client? What type of system did they want you to design?**

The client for this project was DriverPass, a company that wanted to help students prepare better for their driving tests. The goal was to design a web-based system where customers could access online classes and practice tests, schedule and manage driving lessons, and track their progress. The system also needed to support DriverPass employees by allowing them to manage customer information, appointments, drivers, vehicles, training packages, and reports. The system would also receive updated rules, policies, and sample questions from the DMV so the training material could remain current.

**What did you do particularly well?**

I think I did particularly well at taking the requirements from DriverPass and showing how the different users would interact with the system. The case and activity diagrams helped me organize the distinct functions and responsibilities of customers and employees. I did an excellent job considering security and access levels because the system contains customer and employee information. Separating access based on user roles helps make sure each person only has access to the information and functions they need.

**If you could choose one part of your work on these documents to revise, what would you pick? How would you improve it?**

If I could revise one part of my work, I would improve the beginning of my business requirements document, especially the system background and objectives. I would make the system components and goals more detailed from the beginning instead of adding more detail as I continued working through the project. I learned that clearly defining the requirements early makes the design process easier because those requirements become the foundation for the diagrams and technical decisions that come later.

**How did you interpret the user's needs and implement them into your system design? Why is it so important to consider the user's needs when designing?**

I interpreted the user's needs by looking at what each type of user needed to accomplish with the system and turning those needs into functional requirements and system interactions. Customers needed to manage appointments, access training materials, and practice tests, and track their progress, while employees needed different capabilities depending on their roles. I used those requirements when creating the UML diagrams and technical requirements for the system. Considering user needs is important because a system can work technically but still fails if it does not solve the problems of the people who use it.

**How do you approach designing software? What techniques or strategies would you use in the future to analyze and design a system?**

My approach to designing software is to first understand the problem the client is trying to solve and then break the system down into smaller requirements and processes. From there, I can identify the users, determine what each user needs to do, and use diagrams to visualize how the system should work before development begins. In the future, I would continue using techniques such as gathering requirements, separating functional and nonfunctional requirements, creating use case, activity, sequence, and class diagrams, and reviewing the design against the original client requirements. This project showed me how important it is to plan and understand a system before beginning development.
